Resources

In recent years, there has been a tremendous demand for atomically flat thin films across a wide range of industries, including electronics, photonics, energy generation and storage, medical, biomedical, and aerospace. Defect-free, conformal, and uniform thin films are essential for ensuring consistent material performance in these applications, and atomically flat films play a vital role in modern nanofabrication.

Atomic layer deposition is one such technique that stands out for several reasons, including precise thickness control, excellent film conformality, low-temperature processing, stoichiometric control, and inherent film quality. The demand for continuous and pinhole-free films in semiconductor devices has further driven developments in ALD.

ALD effectively meets the requirements for atomic-level control and conformal deposition through sequential, self-limiting surface reactions. The primary advantages of ALD include precise thickness control at the Angstrom or monolayer level and excellent step coverage on high aspect ratio structures.

XPS is a surface sensitive analytical technique used to determine elemental composition and chemical states. When a material is irradiated with monochromatic X-rays, core-level electrons are emitted and their kinetic energy is measured. The binding energy is calculated from this measurement, enabling elemental identification and oxidation-state analysis.

X-ray Diffraction is used to investigate crystallographic structure, phase composition, and degree of crystallinity in thin films. By measuring diffraction peaks that satisfy Bragg’s law, interplanar spacing and crystal structure can be identified.

Spectroscopic ellipsometry is a non-destructive optical technique used to determine thin film thickness and optical constants. It analyzes the change in polarization state of reflected light and is highly effective for thin film and nanomaterial characterization.
